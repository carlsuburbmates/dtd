# The Traffic-Light Rule for Fear

## The Setup

<div className="lead text-lg font-light text-muted-foreground">

Your dog sees something unfamiliar such as a skateboard, child, umbrella, vacuum, grooming brush, car, statue, or person wearing a hat. Sometimes they seem curious, sometimes hesitant, and sometimes they bark, hide, or panic.

</div>

## What Matters Here

### What to Notice

<ChecklistBlock items={["The same experience can be safe or unsafe depending on the dog's emotional state in that moment.", "A green-yellow-red filter helps the owner decide whether to continue, make it easier, or stop."]} variant="checklist" />

> **Common Mistake:** Turning yellow into red by pushing a dog that is already unsure because they are almost doing it.

### What to Watch

- Green usually means calm looking, eating, sniffing, approaching and retreating, loose movement, and quick recovery.
- Yellow often means pausing, leaning away, lowering the body, lip licking, yawning, moving behind the owner, hesitating on food, or needing more distance.
- Red includes freezing, refusing food, trembling, repeated barking, growling, snapping, trying to escape, hiding, panic, or failure to recover.

> **Rule for this lesson:** Stay in green. Respect yellow. Exit red. If the experience is no longer easy enough for the dog, step back.

<MediaEmbed src="/media/mod_04.mp4" caption="Media moment illustrating the core concept." />

## What to Do Now

- If the dog is green, reward calm observation, keep the session short, and finish on a good note.
- If the dog is yellow, increase distance, lower intensity, reduce movement or noise, allow retreat, reward recovery, and end soon.
- If the dog is red, stop, move away, do not punish, do not keep exposing, allow recovery, record what happened, and seek help if the pattern is repeated or unsafe.

## When to Get Help

> **Safety Check:** Seek support if the dog regularly goes straight to red, has repeated red reactions to normal life, cannot recover after distance, is becoming more fearful over time, shows growling, snapping, lunging, or biting, or feels unsafe around children, visitors, dogs, or public spaces.

## Your Next Step

Track the exposure type, distance, traffic-light colour, first body signal, whether food was accepted, recovery time, what helped, and what should be avoided next time.
